By the middle of the eighteenth century, the port had become the busiest on the northern coast. Ships arrived with timber and left with salt, and the merchants who handled both built the tall, narrow houses that still line the quay today.
What the records do not show is how the town fed itself. The fields behind the harbour were thin and stony, and most of the grain came by sea. A bad winter in the shipping lanes meant a hungry spring on land.
This is the pattern we will follow throughout the series: a place, a trade, and the people who depended on both without ever quite controlling either.
