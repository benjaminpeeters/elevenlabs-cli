This is a five-minute breathing practice in two parts: a few minutes of cyclic sighing to settle the body, then three rounds of slower breathing with a short hold. Sit or lie down, whichever lets the belly move freely.
The cyclic sigh is simple. Two inhales through the nose, the second one short, topping up the first; then one long, slow exhale through the mouth, as if fogging a mirror. The exhale is where the calm comes from, so let it be longer than the inhale.
Let us try one. Breathe in through the nose. And a little more. Now, slowly, all the way out through the mouth. Good. Again, in your own time. In through the nose. Top it up. And a long, slow exhale. Continue like this for the next minute; I will stay quiet.
Now let the breath return to normal for a few cycles. Nothing to do. Notice the hands, the feet, the weight of the body. The mind may already feel a little quieter.
Part two. Breathe in through the nose for four. Breathe out through the mouth for six. And now hold, with empty lungs, for as long as is comfortable, no strain. When the urge to breathe arrives, take a gentle breath in, and let the breathing settle again.
That is one round. We will do two more, each hold a little longer if it feels right, a little shorter if it does not. There is no target. The point is to notice the urge, let it be there for a moment, and answer it calmly.
In for four. Out for six. And hold. Stay soft in the face and the shoulders. When you need to, breathe in, and rest.
Last round. In for four. Out for six. Hold, and rest in the stillness. When the breath returns, let it be quiet and slow. Then open the eyes, and notice how the room looks now.
