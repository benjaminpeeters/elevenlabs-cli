Non-sleep deep rest, or NSDR, is the name given to a family of practices that put the body into a state close to sleep while the mind stays awake. Yoga nidra is the oldest of them; a ten-minute guided track is all it takes.
What it does is measurable. Heart rate and breathing slow, the stress hormones fall, and in one study dopamine in the part of the brain that plans movement rose by about sixty percent after a single session.
The practical use is simple: after a bad night, do a ten or twenty-minute session in the early afternoon instead of a coffee. You will not sleep, but you will get some of what sleep gives, and you will not pay for it at bedtime.
