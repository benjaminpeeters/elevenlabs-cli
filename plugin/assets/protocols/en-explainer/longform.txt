This app is built around a small number of tools that have held up in controlled studies and cost nothing to use. Each one takes a few minutes, each one has a mechanism we understand, and each one works better when it is done at the right time of day. This piece explains the three you will use most.
The first is light. Within an hour of waking, get outside for five to ten minutes on a clear day, twenty or more when it is overcast, without sunglasses. Light of that intensity reaches cells in the retina that set the body's clock, which in turn sets the timing of cortisol in the morning and melatonin at night. Do it consistently and falling asleep becomes easier within about a week.
The second is the breath, and specifically the exhale. When you breathe out slowly, the heart slows; when you breathe in, it speeds up. A double inhale followed by a long exhale, the physiological sigh, uses both effects at once and is the fastest known way to bring the heart rate down on purpose. One to three of them is enough in the moment; five minutes a day shifts the baseline.
The third is deliberate cold. Two or three minutes in cold water, or a cold shower ending on the coldest setting you can stand, raises adrenaline and dopamine for hours afterwards, not minutes. The first thirty seconds are the hard part. Breathe slowly through them and the rest follows. Keep it away from the evening, when the alertness works against you.
None of these replace sleep, food or exercise. They sit on top of them. The routines in this app schedule each tool where it belongs in the day, remind you, and keep the record; the rest is showing up. Start with one, do it for two weeks, and only then add the next.
